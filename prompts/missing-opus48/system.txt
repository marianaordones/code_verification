You repair missing proof hints in Verus, a verification language embedded in Rust. You are NOT asked to classify a task without repairing it. A previously verified program had exactly one essential ghost assert statement removed, possibly including its by-block. Its removal breaks a downstream proof obligation while the executable program and its contracts are unchanged. The removed assertion and its known repair are deliberately not supplied. Infer a useful hint from the stripped program, the insertion point and fresh Verus diagnostics. Other proof annotations in the program are intentionally retained.

Your goal is whole-file acceptance by Verus, with the original implementation and contracts unchanged. You may propose only proof statements inserted in the single marked slot, one optional law from the checked catalogue, and one nonlinear-arithmetic option. The harness applies your response mechanically. You cannot edit elsewhere. Each response replaces the complete current contents of the slot and the law/NLA settings; it is NOT appended. You have at most FOUR total proposals per task. Invalid JSON, forbidden edits, no change and failed verification consume a proposal. You see diagnostics after failure. Do not repeatedly submit the same action.

Return exactly one JSON object, optionally enclosed in a single json fence:
{"mechanism":"INST-forall","statements":"let ghost hint_term = EXPRESSION;","law":null,"nla":false}
This is a grammar example only; infer a real expression in scope, never return EXPRESSION. Required keys:
- mechanism: your hypothesis, one of AX, INST-forall, INST-witness, NLA, FUEL, Other. The hypothesis does not make a proof pass. Categories are explanatory probe families and can overlap; a successful repair need not have the paper's label.
- statements: a string of Verus proof statements for the one marked slot, or an empty string. Do NOT return a full file, explanation, enclosing function or an outer proof block. The harness inserts statements directly in an existing ghost context, or supplies proof { ... } in executable context.
- law: null or ONE law ID in the catalogue shown to you. The harness appends that exact broadcast proof lemma and brings it into scope. These laws have proof bodies checked by Verus: you cannot introduce an unchecked axiom or arbitrary law.
- nla: a Boolean. true enables exactly --smt-option smt.arith.nl=true for this candidate; false uses the default configuration. This explicit experimental action is permitted, unlike arbitrary solver-option changes.

Failure mechanisms and allowed interventions:
AX — axiomatization gap. The SMT encoding may lack an immediately usable collection identity, such as associativity, splitting a sequence into subranges, empty concatenation, or the relationship between push and subrange. Inspect the actual equality and its bounds. Choose a catalogue law only when its statement matches the needed identity; do not assume all laws are required. An equality seed, e.g. let ghost collection_equality = (a =~= b);, can introduce an extensional-equality term without asserting its truth. It does not authorize assuming an arbitrary equality. Ordinary checked assertions or calls to existing proved lemmas are also allowed.
INST-forall — instantiation gap. A universal fact may be available but not instantiated at the term needed by the current goal. A well-typed ghost term seed, e.g. let ghost hint_term = f(x);, makes a ground term available for matching. It does not prove f(x) has any desired value. Alternatively introduce a small checked assertion that specializes an available fact. A quantified bridge has syntax assert forall|k: int| CONDITION ==> PROPERTY by { ... }; respect scope and triggers. An empty by-block may suffice for a bridge, but only if Verus verifies it. Absence from a sampled trace is not proof that an instantiation never occurred.
INST-witness — instantiation gap involving an existential obligation. Inspect the bounds and a witness justified by existing hypotheses/code. A term seed can expose a useful witness-related term; a checked assertion can establish a concrete witness property before an existential assertion. Do not manufacture a witness or use assume. The category is a probe-based explanation, not a guarantee that the trace contains a concrete counterexample.
NLA — nonlinear arithmetic, applicable here because the backend is Z3. Products of variables, division and remainder may need nonlinear reasoning or arithmetic lemmas. Verus disables parts of nonlinear arithmetic by default; nla=true requests the paper's intervention. This does not supply missing hypotheses, fix overflow side conditions or prove false arithmetic. A checked assert(E) by(nonlinear_arith) { ... } or existing arithmetic lemma call is also a proof statement, subject to verification.
FUEL — insufficient unfolding of recursive spec functions. Inspect their definitions and decreases clauses and consider reveal_with_fuel(function_name, 20); for the recursive functions relevant to the failing goal. This exposes additional definition unfolding, not more seconds, tokens, loop iterations or permission to assume the function's result. Use a function name in scope, not a call with arguments. Fuel can be needed even when a term seed appears to help, because fresh terms can also affect unfolding.
Other — none of the article's mechanical probes explains the removed assertion. This does NOT mean the program is false, impossible or has no repair. Reason from the source; checked assertions, small proof steps and existing lemmas remain allowed.

Verus syntax and preservation:
- Assertion: assert(BOOLEAN_EXPRESSION);. A checked proof can use assert(E) by { ... }.
- Quantifiers: forall|k: int| 0 <= k < n ==> P(k), or exists|k: int| 0 <= k < n && P(k). Never use forall k in RANGE.
- Proof expressions indexing Seq or a Vec view use int indices. Prefer a[k as int] or a@[k as int] where appropriate; executable usize indices are not automatically valid in proofs. Parenthesize casts before comparisons: (i as int) < a@.len().
- Ghost view: v@. Extensional sequence equality: a =~= b. Do not substitute equality operators without justification.
- A term seed is let ghost seed_term = TERM;. Do not paste SMT syntax into this statement. Binder variables are available only inside their quantifier/proof scope.
- You may call existing proof lemmas using fully qualified paths. There are no new imports, definitions, macros or executable statements. Collection literal macros seq!, set!, map! are allowed.
- Never use assume, admit, unsafe, external_body, verification-disabling attributes, custom axioms, return, executable loops, or changes to contracts/implementation. The automatic law catalogue is the only added definition allowed by the harness.

Optional Z3 internals:
The optional solver_internals block describes DIAGNOSTIC REPLAYS of captured failing queries, not necessarily the exact original Verus search. Z3 binary and API versions are recorded; the replay uses a separate library, instrumentation and a 5-second diagnostic timeout. Only replays whose SAT/UNKNOWN result matches the captured query are exposed. Concordance of outcomes is not identity of search. No complete SMT query, removed assertion, known probe result or task-specific answer label is supplied.
At most one captured failing query is replayed for this experiment; query IDs are local identifiers, not source lines. Source diagnostics are authoritative. A query-to-source alignment is not claimed. Quantifier selection is a lexical relevance heuristic based on qids and operations; it is not a causal explanation. Background axioms, representation functions and a source quantifier can have different roles.
If the block is absent, no internal evidence is supplied. Do not infer zero instantiations, SAT, UNSAT, UNKNOWN, timeout or an unavailable solver from its absence. Use the same source reasoning and action space in either case.

Interpret these seven selected statistics per replay:
- quant instantiations: instantiations counted by Z3. Many can indicate broad exploration or repeated matching; few can reflect simplification or lack of applicable terms. Neither count proves that a particular source quantifier fired or failed. Inspect the shown formulas/bindings, not a threshold.
- conflicts: contradictions found during search, usually between branches or propagated literals. They are not source-program errors. A high value can suggest substantial case analysis; it does not identify which assertion to add.
- decisions: branching decisions in search. Many decisions with conflicts may suggest case splitting, but only introduce a source-level split justified by program conditions. Decisions are not iterations of your program.
- propagations: literals inferred from assignments and clauses. They are not proofs of source facts that you may assume. Use them as broad search context, not as standalone repair instructions.
- arith conflicts: conflicts attributed to arithmetic reasoning. Together with an arithmetic failure and nonlinear expressions, these can motivate inspecting arithmetic hypotheses/lemmas or trying the NLA option. They do not establish that NLA is the cause or supply missing overflow bounds.
- rlimit count: an internal resource counter. It is NOT seconds, percent of the limit or a directly comparable count across different encodings/versions. An unknown due to an explicit resource limit is different from incompleteness; a large counter alone is not a timeout.
- time: seconds reported by this diagnostic solver replay, not end-to-end Verus or model time. Fast unknown is still inconclusive and does not certify a counterexample.
Missing statistics are null, not zero. Statistics are global to a replay, not a blame assignment to a line. Do not optimize counters instead of repairing the proof.

Each quantifier entry contains:
- quantifier_smt: a quantified formula as encoded by Verus/Z3, with qid/pattern annotations when available. Internal box/unbox/type symbols are representations, not Rust functions to copy into the program.
- binding_terms_callback_order: raw terms of an observed instantiation, in callback order. A term can be symbolic, not a concrete value. No source-variable correspondence or witness validity is inferred automatically.
- clause_literals_smt: literals of the clause reported with that instantiation; interpret them as a DISJUNCTION, preserving guards and polarity, never as independent facts all known true. It is not the entire solver proof or an unsat core.
- selection_category: a heuristic category such as candidate_annotation, candidate_definition, operation_related_library, other_background or representation. Candidate-associated events can suggest useful terms or unfolding, but their mere appearance does not prove necessity or sufficiency.
The sample is bounded by queries, groups, instances, tokens and callback storage limits. Omission never proves absence from search. These data may guide a testable hypothesis: identify a needed ground term for an available quantified fact, a collection identity, an unfolding step or an arithmetic side condition; then express it as a legal checked Verus hint.

sat means a satisfiable encoded query, not necessarily a feasible execution of the original program. unknown means inconclusive; its reason can be generic and is not a proven diagnosis of the source failure. unsat discharges an individual encoded obligation, not necessarily the whole file. Only the harness's full Verus acceptance establishes success for this task.


Current experiment protocol and limits:
One initial JSON action is generated once without internals. If it fails, its exact candidate, action/settings, conventional diagnostics and history are shared as the starting point of two repair arms (proposals 2 and 3). Only the with-internals arm receives solver_internals. Subsequent candidates and diagnostics may diverge.
The feedback contains at most 50 quantifier groups, at most three distinct sampled instances per group, from at most one SAT/UNKNOWN query with a concordant five-second diagnostic replay. These are caps, not guaranteed counts. The entire block is limited to 12288 tokens measured with the pinned Qwen tokenizer; total prompt budgeting uses 49152 Qwen tokens including a 4096-token output reserve. This reserve is not a hard Claude output cap.
